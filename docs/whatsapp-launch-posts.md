# Catch the AI — 5-Day WhatsApp Community Test (ready to paste)

**How to run each day (2 minutes):**
1. Morning (same time daily, e.g. 9:00): paste the *message*, then immediately create the *poll* with the question and options exactly as written.
2. Next morning: paste the *reveal* first (fill in the % from the poll results), then post the new day's message + poll below it.
3. Track streaks: tap poll votes to see who voted; keep a note of who's correct each day. Name-check perfect streaks in Day 3 and Day 5 reveals.
4. Day-1 and Day-5 voter counts are your test metric. Gate: ≥30% of Day-1 voters still voting Day 5 (and ≥20 Day-1 voters for the test to count).

Real or Robot days: the two texts are too long for poll options, so they go in the message labeled A and B — the poll options are just "A" and "B".

## DAY 1 (Monday) — Spot the Slip

**Morning message:**

🎯 *CATCH THE AI — Day 1 of 5*

New game, all this week. Every morning I post something an AI got wrong. First one is easy. They get harder.

An AI assistant summarized this vacation-rental listing. Here's the *original*:

"Cozy Pines Cottage sleeps six across three bedrooms and has two full bathrooms. The nightly rate is $180, with a two-night minimum on weekends. The beach is a ten-minute walk down a marked trail. Pets are not allowed, and smoking is prohibited anywhere on the property. Parking is available for up to two cars."

One sentence in the AI's summary below gets the original wrong. Vote for the one you'd catch. Answer tomorrow morning. 👇

**Poll question:** Which sentence did the AI get wrong?

**Poll options:**
1. Cozy Pines Cottage sleeps six people and has two full bathrooms.
2. It costs $180 per night, with a two-night minimum on weekends.
3. The beach is about a thirty-minute walk away via a marked trail.
4. Pets and smoking are not allowed, and there is parking for two cars.

**Reveal (post Tuesday morning, before Day 2):**

🎯 *Day 1 answer:* Sentence 3. The listing says the beach is a *ten-minute* walk — the AI stretched it to thirty. ___% of you caught it.

💡 *The tell:* AI summaries slip on numbers more than anything else. Before you rely on a figure in any AI summary, re-check it against the original.

## DAY 2 (Tuesday) — Real or Robot

**Morning message:**

🎯 *CATCH THE AI — Day 2 of 5*

One of these two hotel reviews was written by a real person. The other is AI. Which one is the *human*?

*Review A:*
"Nestled in the heart of downtown, this hotel offers the perfect blend of comfort, convenience, and charm. The rooms are spacious and inviting, the staff is friendly and attentive, and the amenities are truly top-notch. Whether you are traveling for business or pleasure, this property delivers a seamless experience from check-in to check-out. The vibrant location puts you steps away from dining, shopping, and entertainment. I would not hesitate to recommend it to anyone visiting the area."

*Review B:*
"We stayed three nights in room 412, which faces the parking garage — ask for the other side if you are a light sleeper, because the garbage trucks come at 5. That said, the beds are genuinely great, and the woman at the front desk (Renata, I think) let us store bags until our 9 p.m. flight and drew us a map to a taco place that turned out to be the best meal of the trip. The elevator smells like the pool. Would stay again."

**Poll question:** Which review did the HUMAN write?

**Poll options:**
1. A
2. B

**Reveal (post Wednesday morning, before Day 3):**

🎯 *Day 2 answer:* B is the human. Room 412, Renata at the front desk, an elevator that smells like the pool — real reviewers give you details you could check. Review A is adjectives in threes ("comfort, convenience, and charm") with words like *nestled*, *seamless*, *vibrant* — and zero specifics. ___% of you spotted the human.

💡 *The tell:* specificity. If a review could describe any hotel on Earth, a machine probably wrote it.

## DAY 4 (Thursday) — Real or Robot

**Morning message:**

🎯 *CATCH THE AI — Day 4 of 5*

One of these apology emails for a missed meeting was written by a real person. Which one?

*Email A:*
"I sincerely apologize for missing our meeting this afternoon. I understand that your time is valuable, and I regret any inconvenience my absence may have caused. Please know that this is not a reflection of my commitment to our work together. I would greatly appreciate the opportunity to reschedule at a time that is convenient for you. Thank you for your patience and understanding, and I look forward to reconnecting soon."

*Email B:*
"I'm so sorry about this afternoon — I had us down for 2:00, not 12:00. I entered it on my phone while boarding a train, which is apparently something I should never do again. You waited twenty minutes and that is on me. I'm free Thursday any time after 10, or Friday before lunch; I'll let you pick since I'm the one who blew it. The draft you sent is good, by the way — comments coming tonight."

**Poll question:** Which email did the HUMAN write?

**Poll options:**
1. A
2. B

**Reveal (post Friday morning, before Day 5):**

🎯 *Day 4 answer:* B. A real apology contains the embarrassing details — 2:00 vs 12:00, entered on a train, "you waited twenty minutes and that is on me." Email A apologizes for "any inconvenience" without ever saying what happened. ___% of you got it.

💡 *The tell:* abstraction. Machines apologize in categories; humans apologize in specifics.

Final round below — this is the hardest kind of AI mistake there is. 👇
